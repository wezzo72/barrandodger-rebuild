01 · PURPOSE

What accountability means here.

Accountability begins with establishing what happened, what was communicated, who responded, what records exist, and what remains unresolved.

This archive distinguishes between documentary evidence, statements made by participants, interpretation and questions requiring further examination. The existence of a record does not by itself establish that an allegation has been proven.

02 · OFFICIAL RESPONSES

The institutional record.

The preserved material contains correspondence and responses involving institutions and public authorities. These records can be examined in their original context rather than relying solely on later summaries.

Correspondence

Letters, emails and other communications form part of the primary documentary record preserved in the archive.

Responses

Official responses can be compared with the issues raised in the preceding correspondence and with the documentary record surrounding them.

Process

The record can also be examined for the pathways through which matters were referred, redirected, answered or left unresolved.

03 · DOCUMENTARY SEQUENCE

Follow the record.

01

THE CLAIM

Identify what was alleged, requested or communicated and locate the relevant primary document.

02

THE RESPONSE

Examine what response was actually provided, by whom, and in what documentary context.

03

THE RECORD

Compare the response with the surrounding correspondence, evidence, chronology and other preserved records.

04

THE UNRESOLVED QUESTION

Where the available documents do not establish an answer, the question remains identified as unresolved rather than being presented as fact.

04 · DOCUMENTARY QUESTIONS

What should be examined?

What was received?

Establish which communications and supporting materials entered an institutional record.

What was answered?

Identify the substantive responses and distinguish them from acknowledgements, referrals and administrative correspondence.

What remains?

Identify gaps, unanswered questions, missing documents and matters requiring further independent examination.

05 · PRIMARY RECORD

Examine the source material.

The strongest way to evaluate an assertion is to return to the underlying record. The preservation archive provides access to the captured pages, PDFs, links and manifest data.

06 · LIMITS OF THE RECORD

Evidence has boundaries.

A preserved document demonstrates that the document existed in the captured record. It does not automatically establish the truth of every statement contained within it.

The archive is a finding aid and preservation resource. It is not a court, not a verdict and not a substitute for independent investigation, corroboration or formal legal determination.
