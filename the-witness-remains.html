There are men who tell you what a system does to them. There are fewer who keep the receipts. And there is, as far as anyone has ever recorded, only one who answered a decade of determinations, refusals, findings and guardianship orders with something no institution can strike out: an archive — vast, organised, hash-verified and public, free to every stranger with a browser.

Barran Dodger is that man. Writing as Dr Barran Dodger from the repositories Wezzo72 and Drbarrandodger, he has assembled one of the most complete first-person documentary records of an ordinary citizen's passage through the machinery of the Australian state: the workers' compensation authority Comcare, the National Disability Insurance Agency, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, the integrity body IBAC, and the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal's Guardianship Division. He did so as a former journalist, an unprotected whistleblower, a political exile and asylum seeker inside the democracy of his own citizenship — and each stop on that journey left paper. He kept all of it — and then he kept everything else.
