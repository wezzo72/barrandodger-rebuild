3. Robust Probability Framework and Epistemic Categories

Following the archive’s own distinctions, the Forensic Integrity Protocol’s A/B/C/D/H classification, and the refined robust probability layer for intellectual and forensic fortification:

  • Practically undeniable digital existence (near-certainty, P ≈ 1 for the artefact under independent verification): The presence of a specific file with a matching SHA-256 hash, corroborated by Git commit history and (where present) OpenTimestamps, establishes that the exact byte sequence existed in the repository at the time of the commit or timestamp. This is a fact about the digital artefact, not about external events. Probability of post-seal tampering approaches zero when independent re-computation matches, grounded in SHA-256 collision resistance (≈ 2-128). This layer is what the fortification makes robust, factually established, and practically undeniable for the preserved record. The interactive verifier operationalises this check for any examiner. The entire archive is fortified to this standard for sealed artefacts. Multiple independent paths further strengthen this.
  • Documented fact / official record (high probability of existence and authenticity of the paper, P high once primary examined): Letters, receipts, administrative decisions on agency letterheads. The paper exists and was produced by the issuing body. Probability that the document was created as described is high once the original or certified copy is examined and cross-checked against the sealed digital copy. Classified A under the Protocol where fully inspectable.
  • First-person account / testimony (medium-to-high probability of sincere belief; variable probability of correspondence to external events): The author’s contemporaneous statements. Integrity of the digital record is high (near-certainty of the text as sealed); whether the described events occurred as stated requires corroboration. The sealed record makes the testimony itself examinable without alteration risk. Often D under the Protocol.
  • Allegation / inference / interpretation (low-to-medium prior probability pending independent investigation): Conclusions drawn from the record (e.g., coordinated targeting, specific criminal characterisations). These remain open questions. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence; the archive supplies the documentary base for that examination rather than a final determination. Bayesian updating is possible by any examiner who weighs the sealed primary materials. High-consequence (H) claims stay labelled as such.
  • Spiritual belief / prophetic framing: Categorically distinct from empirical proof. Preserved as part of the author’s testimony and ministry, not as forensic evidence. Its digital integrity is sealed (near-certainty); its status as belief is explicitly labelled.

This stratification increases intellectual honesty and legal defensibility: the archive does not collapse distinct epistemic layers. The archive is a public finding aid and documentary repository. It preserves records so that they may be examined by any competent authority, journalist, researcher, or member of the public. Nothing on this site or in the repository constitutes:

  • An allegation that has been proven beyond reasonable doubt or on the balance of probabilities in any tribunal;

5. How to Independently Verify

  1. Download any file from the repository or live site.
  2. Compute its SHA-256 using the in-browser tool above, or locally (e.g., sha256sum filename.pdf).
  3. Compare against the published hash or companion .sha256 file.
  4. For OpenTimestamps: upload the file and .ots proof to a public verifier.
  5. Cross-check dates against Git commit history and any external timestamps.
  6. Examine the underlying official documents (where linked) rather than secondary summaries.
  7. Confirm the completeness workflow status on the latest commit (visible in GitHub Actions tab). Note recent failures indicate count deviation from the sealed 660 baseline — this is transparent monitoring.
  8. Apply the A/B/C/D/H classification from the linked Forensic Integrity Protocol when assessing individual items.
  9. Recompute and match hashes to confirm the robust probability of digital integrity approaches practical certainty under cryptographic assumptions (SHA-256 bound ≈ 2-128).
  10. Apply the multi-layer protocol: confirm local hash + Git history + workflow status + timestamps where available for reinforced practical certainty.
