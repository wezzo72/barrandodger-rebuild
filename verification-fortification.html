3. Robust Probability Framework and Epistemic Categories

Following the archive’s own distinctions, and adding a qualitative probability layer for intellectual fortification:

  • Undeniable digital existence (near-certainty / practically certain): The presence of a specific file with a matching SHA-256 hash, corroborated by Git commit history and (where present) OpenTimestamps, establishes that the exact byte sequence existed in the repository at the time of the commit or timestamp. This is a fact about the digital artefact, not about the world outside it. Probability of tampering after the seal approaches zero for practical purposes when independent re-computation matches.
  • Documented fact / official record (high probability of existence and authenticity of the paper): Letters, receipts, administrative decisions on agency letterheads. The paper exists and was produced by the issuing body. Probability that the document was created as described is high once the original or certified copy is examined and cross-checked against the sealed digital copy.
  • First-person account / testimony (medium-to-high probability of sincere belief; variable probability of correspondence to external events): The author’s contemporaneous statements. Integrity of the digital record is high; whether the described events occurred as stated requires corroboration. The sealed record makes the testimony itself examinable without alteration risk.
  • Allegation / inference / interpretation (low-to-medium prior probability pending independent investigation): Conclusions drawn from the record (e.g., coordinated targeting, specific criminal characterisations). These remain open questions. Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence; the archive supplies the documentary base for that examination rather than a final determination. Bayesian updating is possible by any examiner who weighs the sealed primary materials.
  • Spiritual belief / prophetic framing: Categorically distinct from empirical proof. Preserved as part of the author’s testimony and ministry, not as forensic evidence. Its digital integrity is sealed; its status as belief is explicitly labelled.

This stratification increases intellectual honesty and legal defensibility: the archive does not collapse distinct epistemic layers. The digital corpus is fortified to a standard of practical undeniability of its contents as of the seal dates; substantive probability assessments remain the domain of independent examination.

4. Legal Fortification Statement

The archive is a public finding aid and documentary repository. It preserves records so that they may be examined by any competent authority, journalist, researcher, or member of the public. Nothing on this site or in the repository constitutes:

  • A court finding, judicial determination, or administrative decision;
  • An allegation that has been proven beyond reasonable doubt or on the balance of probabilities in any tribunal;
  • A substitute for independent investigation by appropriate bodies (police, courts, ombudsmen, human-rights commissions, etc.).

Where official documents are cited, the primary source is the document itself. Readers are directed to the original files, their hashes, and the Git history for chain-of-custody verification. This structure maximises transparency and minimises the risk of mischaracterisation. The fortification layer itself is under version control and subject to the same integrity mechanisms.

5. How to Independently Verify

  1. Download any file from the repository or live site.
  2. Compute its SHA-256 (e.g., sha256sum filename.pdf or equivalent).
  3. Compare against the published hash or companion .sha256 file.
  4. For OpenTimestamps: upload the file and .ots proof to a public verifier.
  5. Cross-check dates against Git commit history and any external timestamps.
  6. Examine the underlying official documents (where linked) rather than secondary summaries.
  7. Confirm the completeness workflow has passed on the latest commit (visible in GitHub Actions).
