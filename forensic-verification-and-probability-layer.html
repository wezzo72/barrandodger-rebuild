Forensic Verification & Probability Layer

FINDING AID · NOT A COURT · NOT A VERDICT · APPEARANCE IS NOT A FINDING

This layer is added to the Barran Dodger / Dr Richard William McLean public documentary archive to increase the forensic, intellectual, and documentary fortification of the record. It distinguishes categories of material so that readers can assess probability, provenance, and what is independently examinable.

Purpose: To make the archive more robust against dismissal by clearly separating:

  • Documentary records produced by third parties (agencies, courts, correspondence on official letterheads)
  • Personal testimony and first-person narrative
  • Interpretive, theological, or prophetic writings
  • What remains open to independent examination or verification

Nothing on this page constitutes a finding of liability, wrongdoing, or fact. Readers must examine the underlying documents.

1. Categories of Material in the Archive

Documentary / Official Records: Files containing agency letterheads, case numbers, dates, and outcomes generated by government bodies, courts, or other institutions. These are the primary examinable record. Examples include the Official Government Documents Register, SHA-256 hashed PDFs, and correspondence chains. Their existence and content can be verified by downloading and inspecting the files themselves.
Personal Testimony: First-person accounts, statements, and narratives by Dr Richard William McLean / Barran Dodger describing events, experiences, and interpretations. These are presented for examination alongside the documentary record. They are not self-authenticating.
Interpretive / Spiritual / Prophetic Writings: Gospels, prophetic papers, theological reflections, and ministry materials. These express the author's understanding and faith perspective. They are distinct from documentary evidence.

2. Strengthening Factual Undeniability Through Provenance

The archive already employs several fortification methods:

  • SHA-256 hashes published with many documents
  • GitHub commit history and permanent public hosting
  • Multiple mirrors and preservation copies
  • Explicit disclaimers that the sites are finding aids, not courts or verdicts

This layer adds an explicit probability-oriented reading guide: the more independent documentary sources (official letters, case numbers, agency responses) that align on a given point, the higher the baseline for further examination. Absence of rebuttal documents in the public record is noted where relevant, but does not by itself establish any conclusion.

3. Suggested Examination Protocol

  1. Locate the primary document via the Official Government Documents Register or GitHub links.
  2. Verify the SHA-256 hash if provided.
  3. Read the agency's own words, dates, and case identifiers.
  4. Distinguish what the document states from any surrounding interpretation.
  5. Note any absence of corresponding agency responses or findings in the published record.

4. Limitations and Ongoing Work

This layer does not claim completeness of the entire archive. The documentary record is large and continues to be organised. Independent verification by third parties remains the appropriate next step for any specific allegation. The author invites such examination.
