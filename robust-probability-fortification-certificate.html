This certificate addresses only the integrity, existence, and examinability of specific digital byte sequences that have been hash-sealed. Matching a published SHA-256 establishes, under standard cryptographic assumptions, that the exact file content existed in that form at the time of sealing/commit. It does not establish the truth, accuracy, or legal effect of any allegation, interpretation, or narrative contained inside the file. Epistemic categories remain stratified exactly as set out in the Forensic Integrity Protocol (A/B/C/D/H) and the Forensic Probability & Verification Framework.

1. Cryptographic Foundations for Practical Undeniability

Under the random-oracle model and the collision resistance of SHA-256:

  • Targeted second-preimage or collision search against a specific published hash requires approximately \(2^{128}\) work.
  • For any computationally bounded adversary the probability of producing an undetected altered file that still matches the published hash is negligible (on the order of \(2^{-128}\) or lower).
  • When independent recomputation of the SHA-256 matches the published value, Git commit history corroborates the addition, and (where present) an OpenTimestamps receipt anchors the hash, the joint residual risk of post-seal undetected alteration falls well below \(10^{-40}\) under ordinary multi-layer assumptions.

This yields practical certainty (P ≈ 1) of the digital artefact’s existence and byte-for-byte content as of the seal. It is a statement about the sealed file, not about external events.

2. Multi-Layer Independent Confirmation Protocol

  1. Local hash recomputation — in-browser (below) or via standard tools (sha256sum, certutil, etc.).
  2. Git chain-of-custody — public commit history shows when each file was added or altered.
  3. Automated monitoring — growth-aware verify-archive.yml workflow reports counts and alerts only on drops below the sealed 660-record baseline.
  4. OpenTimestamps — where a companion .ots exists, the hash is Bitcoin-anchored.
  5. Cross-archive checks — main archive (wezzo72/Barrandodger) and rebuild mirror.

Any single layer is already strong; the combination produces the residual-risk bound above.

3. Epistemic Stratification (unchanged)

  • Practically undeniable digital existence — sealed artefacts under the bounds in §1.
  • Documented fact / official record — high-confidence existence of the document; content accuracy remains subject to ordinary examination (typically classification A).
  • First-person account — records what the author stated; correspondence to external events is open (often D).
  • Allegation / interpretation — requires independent investigation; high-consequence claims (H) are not treated as established.
  • Spiritual belief — preserved as testimony, not empirical proof.
