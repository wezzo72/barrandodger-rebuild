1. Cryptographic Foundations for Practical Undeniability

Under the random-oracle model and the collision resistance of SHA-256:

  • Targeted second-preimage or collision search against a specific published hash requires approximately 2128 work.
  • For any computationally bounded adversary the probability of producing an undetected altered file that still matches the published hash is negligible (on the order of 2-128 or lower).
  • When independent recomputation of the SHA-256 matches the published value, Git commit history corroborates the addition, and (where present) an OpenTimestamps receipt anchors the hash, the joint residual risk of post-seal undetected alteration falls well below 10-40 under ordinary multi-layer assumptions (local hash + Git + optional OTS + cross-archive + automated monitoring).

This yields practical certainty (P ≈ 1) of the digital artefact’s existence and byte-for-byte content as of the seal. It is a statement about the sealed file, not about external events. Residual risk is dominated by the cryptographic assumption itself and the completeness of the published hash inventory. Operational transparency is itself part of the forensic record. This aligns with standard digital evidence principles in which a cryptographic hash functions as a digital fingerprint establishing continuity of integrity.
