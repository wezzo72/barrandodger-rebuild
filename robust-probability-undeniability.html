2. Robust Probabilistic Reasoning

The pattern documented across more than thirty years and dozens of Australian agencies — consistent refusals, non-allocations, denials of ordinary protections, and administrative outcomes recorded on the agencies’ own letterheads — has extremely low prior probability under an assumption of independent chance or isolated bureaucratic error.

In legal evidence scholarship the “doctrine of chances” and related probabilistic approaches consider whether the observed frequency of similar outcomes exceeds what would be expected for an innocent or ordinary sequence of events. Here the “similar outcomes” are the repeated documented failures of ordinary protection for the same individual. The archive supplies the raw counts and the primary documents for any independent analyst to model this probability.

This is not a calculation of guilt or innocence. It is an invitation to quantify the improbability of the observed multi-agency paper trail arising by coincidence alone. The public record allows that quantification.
