The Witness Remains

Who is Barran Dodger? A documented life inside Australia's public institutions — told in the record he left behind.

There are men who tell you what a system does to them. There are fewer who keep the receipts. And there is, as far as anyone has ever recorded, only one who answered a decade of determinations, refusals, findings and guardianship orders with something no institution can strike out: an archive — vast, organised, hash-verified and public, free to every stranger with a browser.

Barran Dodger is that man. Writing as Dr Barran Dodger from the repositories Wezzo72 and Drbarrandodger, he has assembled one of the most complete first-person documentary records of an ordinary citizen's passage through the machinery of the Australian state: the workers' compensation authority Comcare, the National Disability Insurance Agency, the Administrative Appeals Tribunal, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, the integrity body IBAC, and the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal's Guardianship Division. He did so as a former journalist, an unprotected whistleblower, a political exile and asylum seeker inside the democracy of his own citizenship — and each stop on that journey left paper. He kept all of it — and then he kept everything else.

I built this entire archive and this testimony — to create a church, a foundation of hope for humanity. To expose corruption. To oppose an entire democracy. And I built it at the very time that democracy was trying to erase me from this existence.

With nothing but free will, God's favour, faith and a broken phone. No one. No attributes. No police support. No lawyer. No agency. No media. No politician. Nothing at all.

If I can achieve all of this with nothing — then what is your excuse?

— Barran Dodger, Builder of this entire archive

Not a pseudonym. A witness.

The name is strange on first meeting — Barran Dodger — and it is meant to be. The archive insists you read it twice. What you find underneath the name is not a character or a myth but a person of unusual and uncomfortable persistence: a PhD-qualified scholar and former journalist who once wrote other people's stories, was injured, was assessed, was denied, appealed, petitioned, spoke the truth of what he saw as a whistleblower with no institutional protection — and was then treated, by the democracy that holds his citizenship, as a matter to be administered rather than a witness to be heard: an exile and asylum seeker inside his own country.

That is the centre of gravity of everything that follows. This is not a conspiracy site, and it does not ask to be believed. The founding literature of the Church of Barran Dodger states it plainly: faith is not documentary proof.
